## Background: Morgenstadt and the Amazon

The [Morgenstadt Initiative](https://www.morgenstadt.de/en.html) at Fraunhofer has been working with over 100 partner cities across four continents on sustainable urban development. Its [City Lab methodology](https://www.morgenstadt.de/content/dam/morgenstadt/en/documents/Morgenstadt%20City%20Labs_EN.pdf) combines systems analysis, co-creation workshops, and roadmapping to help cities identify and implement innovation pathways.

Starting in 2024, together with the [Amazonia Institute of Technology, AmIT](https://amazonia4.org/), the [Fundação Getulio Vargas, FGV](https://portal.fgv.br/en) and the [Fraunhofer Liaison Office Brazil](https://www.fraunhofer.org.br/), we began exploring how this methodology could be meaningfully adapted with and for small and medium-sized towns in the Brazilian Amazon. The premise, informed by regional voices and e.g. the [WRI](https://www.wri.org/research/new-economy-brazil-amazon), was that urban development is a precondition for both poverty alleviation and the preservation of the so-called *standing-forest flowing-rivers bioeconomy*. While local motivation in these towns is often high, they often lack the technical capacity and institutional bridges to scale their initiatives into lasting systems.

Exchanges with [Carlos Nobre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Nobre), one of the leading scientists working on the Amazon and the driving force behind Amazonia4.0, as well as with [Adalberto Val](https://twas.org/directory/val-adalberto-luis), Vice President of the Brazilian Academy of Sciences for the Amazon and [Maritta Koch-Weser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritta_Koch-Weser), Programme Coordinator for ["Amazonia em Transformação"](https://www.iea.usp.br/pesquisa/grupos-pesquisa/amazonia-em-transformacao-historia-e-perspectivas) at the [University of São Paulo](https://www5.usp.br/en/), helped shape what became the **Amazonia City Lab Initiative, ACLI**. The initiative was supported from the start by Fraunhofer IAO's directorate.

## Tightening Scope: Circular Plastics in Novo Airão

The initial framing was broad: How to facilitate applied, locally-driven urban research in Amazonian towns. Over the course of 2024 and 2025, we tightened the scope toward a concrete, fundable pilot project based entirely on mutual outreach and existing local momentum.

The partnership with the municipality of [Novo Airão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_Air%C3%A3o) grew out of this dialogue. With roughly 15,000 to 20,000 residents, it sits on the Rio Negro at the gateway to the [UNESCO World Heritage Anavilhanas Archipelago](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/998/) and south of the [Jaú National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja%C3%BA_National_Park). Novo Airão has proactively established strong linkages with local parties, including 12 indigenous and traditional riverine communities in its direct vicinity, an engaged and formalised "Innovation Secretary" in the municipality and active grassroots initiatives around waste separation and ecotourism. Because of this integrated local ecosystem, Novo Airão and our consortium connected: They were looking for technical and institutional bridges to scale and support their efforts, and we were looking for highly engaged partners.

A collaborative scoping visit to Novo Airão in early 2026 deepened this partnership. Local actors, including eco-hotels and the local recyclables cooperative [COOPCAMARE](https://www.instagram.com/coopcamare/), have already operationalised waste separation. However, they highlighted a critical missing piece to their puzzle: Processing capacity. When too much plastic accumulates, it must be trucked back to the general dump unprocessed, worst-case resulting in a "dump-burn-leak" pathway into the waterways.

In direct response to this locally identified gap, ACLI and the Novo Airão stakeholders co-designed the vision for a community-owned, open-source plastics recycling and upcycling Eco-Factory. The vision is a small-scale facility where commonly recoverable household plastics are sorted, cleaned, shredded, and converted into usable materials and products like pressed sheets and utility items for local services, tourism, and education. Everything is built around [open-source hardware](https://preciousplastic.com/) and documented standard operating procedures, to ensure that the facility can be maintained, owned, and eventually replicated using local skills. What was important for us, is that the project shall not begin with installing equipment, but with community mapping, governance setup, and co-created operating rules to establish a hand-to-hand collaboration on equal terms. Where riverine and traditional communities are involved, participation must follow strict, informed opt-in formats.

## Current Status

In January 2026, we submitted the jointly developed ACLI concept note to the [International Climate Initiative, IKI](https://www.international-climate-initiative.com/en/) of the German Federal Ministry for the Environment and Consumer Protection. Fraunhofer IAO is the consortium lead. FGV and Amazonia 4.0 are implementing partners. The Municipality of Novo Airão acts as non-funded host (due to [IKI regulations](https://www.international-climate-initiative.com/fileadmin/iki/Dokumente/Calls/Themencall/2024/IKI_Thematic_Call_2024_Selection_Criteria.pdf)). The proposed budget is €800,000 over 26 months.

A total of 296 project outlines were submitted in this round. And while the concept has strong endorsement and we all stand behind our ambitions, funding is anything but certain.

Regardless of the outcome, the consortium continues to explore additional funding vehicles alongside our local partners. The underlying question guiding our work remains: how can applied research institutions contribute meaningfully to places where sustainability challenges are most pressing, without replicating patterns of one-directional knowledge transfer?

Also regardless of the outcome, working on this initiative has been one of the most personally rewarding things I’ve done during my time at Fraunhofer IAO.
